A tutorial is learning-oriented. It takes place under the guidance of a tutor, and the pupil owes it only attention. Every other obligation belongs to the writer.

The exercise must be meaningful, completable, logically ordered, and complete enough that the learner meets every tool the skill needs. Each step must produce a visible result, however small. The writer points out what to notice, because a learner concentrating on the action does not notice on their own.

A tutorial gives an experience rather than an argument. It cuts explanation to the minimum and links out. It offers no choices and no alternatives; one path succeeds. It speaks as "we will", and it says what the output should look like.

Reliability is the hard constraint. The absent teacher cannot rescue a learner whose step fails.

A tutorial must inspire confidence. Confidence can only be built up layer by layer, and is easily shaken.
